# CS4928 G06 (P09): Final Results

**Study:** Robredo et al., *Analyzing the Ripple Effects of Refactoring* ([paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-025-10801-3), [Zenodo 15209118 v1.0](https://zenodo.org/records/15209118))
**Repository:** https://github.com/SamSmith2004/cs4928_project. Tags: `m1` (M1_PLAN), `m2` (M2_REPRO / PRE_EVENT_A), `pre_event_b`, `event_b`, `FINAL`

**Results.** Under our preregistered M1 rules, the paper's main finding reproduces and transfers. A case counts as short if it lasts fewer than 10 later refactorings. A family passes if at least 7 of its 12 cases are short and at least 10 match the published values within ±0.02. On RQ1's three projects, 24/24 Move and Rename cases are short. On the held-out projects, 21/24 are short in any23 (RQ2) and 24/24 in commons-numbers (Event A). All 277 recalculated values match the paper, and the 1%, 5% and 10% cut-offs change nothing. Our M1 prediction expected Check 2 to fail, and it was wrong. Running the authors' code unchanged also gives back their six 0.0 values at file moves, so these still match (`M2/log.md`, `EventA/results.md`).

**Design decision.** Those six zeros were our Week 9 design pressure. We kept the authors' calculation unchanged and documented the limitation, because editing it would mean we were no longer testing their method. The code's structure supports this choice. `get_file_content` hides a failed read as 0% remaining code, the paths only work on Windows, and our M2 scripts are copied into Event A. An in-place fix would therefore mean editing the code under test, in several copies.

**Event B.** Following moved files showed that the problem is bigger than we had documented. The six zeros are pure renames whose content PyDriller does not return. Read directly from git, they are 0.48–1.00, and the other 271 values match exactly. Tracking also stops at a file's first move, which shortens durations. any23 Move falls from 12/12 short to 11/12 when moved files are followed (median still 5), or 9/12 counting whole refactorings (median 7). No verdict changes, so the Week 9 decision is partly supported. We added read-only audit scripts under `EventB/` and changed no frozen file (`EventB/results.md`).

**Limitations and conclusion.** The main threat is the moved-file problem. We checked only our 72 cases, but 13.5% of `class_id`s in the full dataset have a later path segment, so we make no claim about the paper's overall result. Our samples are small and non-random, and any23 Move's 12 cases come from only 8 refactorings. We checked every AI suggestion we used against the code or data first, such as the Windows path repair in M2 and the whole-refactoring double count in Event B. In our sample, Move and Rename refactorings affect later changes only briefly (69 of 72 cases), with one caveat: moved files make durations look shorter than they are.
